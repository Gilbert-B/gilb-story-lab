# GILB-003 — Ghana's Sleeping Wallets

Final narration script approved by owner (2026-11-12).

Ghana has about 34 million people.

By August 2026, it had 85.8 million registered mobile money accounts.

That's more than twice the population.

But registered accounts are only one way to measure the scale of mobile money.

Look at the transactions.

In August 2026 alone, mobile money processed over one billion transactions.

That's nearly 400 transactions every second, on average.

Clearly, there's a huge amount of activity happening.

Yet how many of those registered accounts are actually active?

As of August 2026, the Bank of Ghana classified 26.4 million of them as active.

That's fewer than one in three.

So what about the other 59.4 million?

The Bank of Ghana considers an account active if it has recorded at least one transaction in the previous 90 days.

Those 59.4 million accounts didn't meet that definition.

That doesn't necessarily mean they've been abandoned.

And here's something else worth looking at.

Between August 2025 and August 2026, registered accounts increased by 8.1 million.

Active accounts increased by 1.3 million.

So in one year, the gap between registered and active accounts widened by almost 7 million.

There's also an important distinction.

85.8 million registered accounts doesn't mean 85.8 million different people.

One person can have several mobile money accounts.

And having an account doesn't necessarily mean using it.

So while registered accounts grew over the past year, active accounts didn't keep pace.

And that raises an important question.

When we talk about the growth of mobile money, should we count how many accounts exist, or how many are actually being used?
