# GILB-006 · "Four Cedis in a Hundred" · Research Pack

Unpacked by GilB · prepared 25 Nov 2026 · consolidates the verification brief (23 Nov 2026), the final script and the production checks.

## 1. What this story is

A short vertical explainer (about 1:50) for the "Unpacked by GilB" series. It tests a familiar claim, "agriculture is the backbone of Ghana's economy", against three numbers from public data:

| Test | Agriculture's share | Period | Verdict shown on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jobs | 37 in every 100 working Ghanaians (37.2%, about 5 million people) | Q3 2025 | "Yes" |
| Output | 23 in every 100 cedis of GDP (22.8%) | 2025 | "Yes" |
| Bank loans | just over 4 in every 100 cedis of banks' outstanding loans (4.3%) | end of 2025 | number only, no tick or cross |

The twist: agriculture is not shrinking. In 2025 it grew 6.8%, faster than the whole economy (6.0%). Bank loans to agriculture also rose (about +6%), but total bank loans rose faster (+16%), so agriculture's share fell from 4.7% to 4.3%.

**Series rules the story follows:** one twist from verifiable public data; no political stance ("this is what the data show"); lay-friendly; neutral wording (no "only", "just" as judgement, "failing", "worrying"); no speculation on causes; distinct visual identity; source lines discreet.

**Working ID:** GILB-006 (proposed; not yet entered in the Story Registry). The voice recording is named GILB007; which ID is correct still needs confirming.

## 4. Computations (shown on screen as "≈" and labelled as computed)

- Dec 2024 gross loans = 111.0 ÷ 1.162 ≈ **GH¢95.5bn**
- Agriculture, Dec 2024 = 4.7% × 95.5 ≈ **GH¢4.49bn**
- Agriculture, Dec 2025 = 4.3% × 111.0 ≈ **GH¢4.77bn**
- Change ≈ **+6%**, against **+16.2%** for all loans
- Rounding check: with the shares anywhere inside their rounding bands (4.65–4.75% and 4.25–4.35%), the agricultural amount still rises (between about +4% and +9%: 4.25/4.75 × 1.162 = 1.040; 4.35/4.65 × 1.162 = 1.087), so "went up" holds; the exact ≈ +6% does not, which is why it is shown with ≈.
- Cross-check: 4.3% of GH¢111.0bn ≈ GH¢4.8bn, which is also the 4.5% of *private-sector* credit (GH¢106.2bn) quoted in the press. Same loans, different denominators.

## 5. Definitions and traps

1. **Loans outstanding, not new lending.** BoG also publishes sector shares of new lending (credit flows). Flows can be negative, so those shares don't add to 100% (mid-2025 press figures add to 109.4%). Never use flow shares.
2. **Total credit vs private-sector credit.** Figure 6.1 gives shares of *all* bank credit (4.3%, Dec 2025). The press figure of 4.5% is a share of private-sector credit. The story uses total credit throughout.
3. **Bank loans are not all farm finance.** Deposit money banks only. Rural and community banks, microfinance, input credit from buyers and outgrower schemes are outside the measure. Say "bank loans", never "money for farmers".
4. **Same sector name.** BoG and GSS both use "agriculture, forestry and fishing" (includes cocoa). Say "agriculture".
5. **Match the years.** Jobs (Q3 2025), GDP (2025) and loans (Dec 2025) all describe 2025.
6. **Two sources for the long trend** (not used in the video): 1999–2023 from a peer-reviewed study (share of private-sector credit); 2024–2026 from BoG charts (share of total credit). The step between them is a change of measure, not a real rise.
7. **Chart readings.** BoG shares were read from chart labels in the Monetary Policy Reports and matched to figures in the text.
8. **Which GDP share.** Confirm whether 22.8% is real or nominal at basic prices.

## 6. Researched but left out (and why)

- **Bad-loan ratios.** Agriculture's NPL ratio: 46.3% (Dec 2025), 65.1% (Jun 2026), 60.9% (Aug 2026). Beside the lending share it reads as the reason banks lend little (a cause), and it moves sharply between reports.
- **Causes** (collateral, risk, interest rates, banks' preference for government securities): off screen; viewers draw their own conclusions.
- **Targets, rules, programmes:** the reported government target of 10% of bank lending by 2028; a 1982 BoG rule that 20% of bank credit go to agriculture; farming programmes.
- **June 2026 share (3.1%)** and the implied fall in cedi terms (3.1% of GH¢124.3bn ≈ GH¢3.9bn): a computed estimate from a chart reading; not used until BoG's own cedi figure is found. The video sticks to 2025.
- **GIRSAL** (state-backed credit-risk guarantee): see §7. Kept out of the core comparison; possibly a story of its own.
- **The 1999–2023 study's argument** (credit-policy reform): numbers only, not its framing. Long-trend series in `GILB006_Agri_Credit_Share_1999_2026.csv`.
- **2021 census** (33% of employed people in agriculture): fallback jobs figure only.

## 7. GIRSAL (researched, not used)

GIRSAL guarantees the lender, not the farmer: up to 70% of principal on an agricultural loan (interest not covered), 12-month terms renewable yearly. Operating since July 2019. As of 30 June 2026 (GIRSAL's site): GH¢943.42m of guarantees to 25 institutions, covering over GH¢1.85bn of loans to 6,695 agribusinesses; 40 participating institutions. BoG gives covered loans a zero risk weight (since 2021) and accepts GIRSAL guarantees as collateral for agricultural loans (from Sept 2025).
Reading notes: average cover ≈ 51% of each loan (943.42 ÷ 1,850, computed). The GH¢1.85bn is cumulative over seven years; BoG's figures are loans outstanding on one date, so don't divide one by the other. Part of GIRSAL's total sits with non-bank lenders. 2024 press figures conflict (GH¢1.18bn vs GH¢1.8bn); use only GIRSAL's dated figures.
